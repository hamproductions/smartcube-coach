## 2. The diagnosis in three numbers

1. Turning rate is not what separates your good and bad solves. You turn at 6.1 per second whenever you are turning, and that is nearly the same in your fastest fifth (6.24) and your slowest fifth (6.05).
2. Pausing is. Your fastest fifth pauses 5.1 s per solve, your slowest fifth 10.5 s. Of the 8.9 s gap between those two groups, 60% is pause time, 36% is extra moves and 4% is turning rate.
3. Move count is second. Fastest fifth 64.5 moves, slowest fifth 84.

Pauses and extra moves come together (a bad pair has both), so these are not independent. The data still points the same way: work on pausing first, move count second, raw speed last.

## 4. Levers

### 4.1 F2L look-ahead (pool: 5.9 s of every F2L; 1.4 s available to your own best tenth)

Evidence:

- Recognition is 4.1 s of a 10.4 s F2L (39%). Pauses in the middle of pairs add 1.8 s.
- Recognition barely shrinks as the cube fills up: medians 0.96, 0.96, 0.96, 0.93 s for pairs 1 to 4 (means 1.11, 1.07, 1.03, 1.00).
- It does not depend on how long the pair is (0.002 s per extra move). A free pair, 4 moves and 0.42 s to execute, still carries 1.05 s of recognition. By case type it ranges from 0.67 to 1.11 s.
- Turning a pair slowly buys about 0.1 s on the next one: after your slowest-turned pairs the next recognition is 0.09 s shorter (interval 0.04 to 0.14) than after your fastest-turned pairs.
- You turn pairs 1 to 3 at 5.6 to 6.2 per second and pair 4 at 8.1.
- In the last layer, OLL execution speed has no relation to PLL recognition (correlation 0.01). There is a small link from a slow last pair to a shorter OLL recognition (-0.13 s, interval -0.22 to -0.04).

Reading: you solve a pair, stop, find the next, solve. Before each of the six recognitions (four pairs, OLL, PLL) the cube is still for a median 0.64 s, and execution proper begins 0.96 s after the previous stage ended.

Drills:

- Slow solves with a hard rule: no stop between pairs. Turn at whatever rate keeps your hands moving, even 2 per second. A stop means the rate was too high. The account has 377 solves in a Slow Solve session, the last on 12 September, and none since.
- Train → look-ahead trainers and pair tracking, on the cube. Train → turn pacer for the fixed-rate slow solves.
- Eyes rule: while executing a pair, do not watch it. Find two pieces of the next pair before the current one is inserted.

Measure: F2L recognition per solve (now 4.1 s, first target 3.3 s, your best tenth 2.7 s). acubemy's fluency percentage is exactly 100 minus the share of time in gaps over 0.4 s (verified on 1000 of 1000 solves), so it tracks this directly: now 55, your fastest fifth 59, first target 60, your best tenth 64.

## 6. Limits of this analysis

- All 1000 solves are one cube over 15 days. The earlier history is on a different cube, so lifetime improvement mixes skill and hardware.
- acubemy's stage boundaries are imperfect: the cross stage often runs one move past the solved cross; 14 solves have the whole F2L counted as cross; its recognition figure ends at the first "real" move, not the first move, in about 40% of pairs. Pause figures computed directly from move timestamps do not depend on those boundaries, which is why this report leans on them.
- Rotations are inferred by acubemy, not sensed. Large cube reorientations in the gyro stream line up with them only weakly above chance (42% of rotations against a 34% baseline), so rotation counts rest on acubemy's reconstruction.
- Gyro data added nothing reliable beyond that: total cube movement mostly tracks solve length, and the gyro and move streams do not cover inspection.
- An inspection duration is stored for every solve although the session has inspection switched off; 53 solves show over 30 s. It was used only as a rough indicator.
- Per-case last-layer numbers come from 5 to 68 executions each. Neighbouring ranks in those lists are not distinguishable.
- Pause behaviour was measured only on these 1000 solves; nothing here says how it changed over the account's life.
